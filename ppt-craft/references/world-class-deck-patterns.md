# World-Class Deck Patterns

## Core Rules

- Audience first: a great deck is a designed change in the audience's mind, not a decorated document.
- One mental model early: install the category, enemy, or core contrast within the first 3-5 slides.
- Proof before decoration: charts, screenshots, cases, demo flows, or real assets must carry credibility.
- Contrast creates memory: before/after, pain/relief, old/new, current/future, chaos/order.
- Every slide needs a job: hook, define, prove, compare, demonstrate, quantify, de-risk, ask.
- Density follows setting: live talk can be sparse; async investor/client decks must be self-contained.
- The deck needs rhythm: alternate dense evidence with low-density vision or synthesis pages.

## Investor Pitch Pattern

Use for fundraising, partnerships, or strategic buy-in.

Common spine:

1. Company purpose in one sentence.
2. Pain/problem and why current solutions fail.
3. Solution and "eureka" insight.
4. Why now.
5. Product or workflow proof.
6. Market/category.
7. Business model or economics.
8. Traction, pilots, or validation.
9. Competition and moat.
10. Team.
11. Ask, use of funds, roadmap.

Sources: Sequoia's business-plan guidance emphasizes company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market, competition, business model, team, financials, and vision. YC's seed deck guidance frames the deck as a clear story told through slides. Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 rule is a useful constraint for live pitch discipline, not a universal page-count law.

Investor deck pass/fail:

- A stranger can repeat the company purpose after slide 1.
- The problem is painful before the solution appears.
- Product proof appears early enough to avoid vapor.
- "Why now" is explicit.
- The ask and use of funds are concrete.

## Strategic/Consulting Pattern

Use for executives, board, strategy, market analysis, transformation, or internal alignment.

Spine:

1. Executive answer first.
2. Current state and stakes.
3. Diagnosis: what drives the issue.
4. Options or scenarios.
5. Recommendation.
6. Evidence and sensitivity.
7. Operating plan.
8. Risks and mitigations.
9. Decisions requested.

Design grammar:

- Page title states the takeaway, not the topic.
- Charts show one message; details live in annotation or appendix.
- Use issue-tree, waterfall, 2x2, timeline, scenario table, and value bridge archetypes.

## Product Keynote Pattern

Use for launches, demos, product vision, and public-facing founder talks.

Spine:

1. Human tension or world shift.
2. Product promise.
3. Demo moment or workflow.
4. Three capability pillars.
5. Proof or user moment.
6. Ecosystem/integration.
7. Future state.
8. Call to action.

Design grammar:

- Big claims, fewer words, product/UI visuals.
- Cinematic hero moments separate sections.
- Motion must express reveal, progress, transformation, or comparison.

## Research/Report Pattern

Use for async reading, industry reports, policy, data studies, and whitepapers.

Spine:

1. Main finding.
2. Method/source credibility.
3. Key insight chapters.
4. Data pages with headline takeaway.
5. Case examples.
6. Implications.
7. Recommendations.
8. Appendix.

Design grammar:

- Higher density is allowed, but hierarchy must be ruthless.
- Use chart + takeaway + evidence note.
- Make source/date/method visible.

## Training/Lesson Pattern

Use for teaching, onboarding, courseware, and workshops.

Spine:

1. Promise and learner outcome.
2. Mental model.
3. Worked example.
4. Practice or scenario.
5. Common mistake.
6. Checklist.
7. Summary and next action.

Design grammar:

- Repeat a stable lesson frame.
- Make progression visible.
- Use examples before abstractions when the topic is unfamiliar.

## Reusable Slide Archetypes

- **Thesis Cover**: name, one-sentence promise, atmosphere image, credentials.
- **Pain Binary**: old world vs new world, surface vs truth, symptom vs root cause.
- **Category Stack**: layers, levels, platform map, missing link.
- **Mechanism Cutaway**: how the system works inside.
- **Evidence Dashboard**: large metric + chart + micro case + source.
- **Future Split**: path without intervention vs path with product.
- **Before/After Intervention**: user's workflow before and after.
- **Moat Map**: data, distribution, model, trust, network, workflow lock-in.
- **Roadmap Timeline**: milestones tied to funding or execution.
- **Ask Page**: amount/decision + use of funds + next milestone + contact/action.
