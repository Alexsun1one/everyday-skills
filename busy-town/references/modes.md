# Modes / 四种模式

The mode is the single most Busy-Town-native decision. Choose it deliberately — the same cooperation can be a panorama (see it all at once), a cutaway (see inside), a two-page spread (compare), or a close-up (zoom in on one role). One mode per image.

## Town Panorama / 全景镇模式

A top-down or high-angle view of an entire town — shops, streets, vehicles, squares, all busy at once. **No perspective vanishing point**; an encyclopedic, even, slightly-flattened layout where every element has equal visual weight but is cleverly categorized by district and street. This is the look that comes from map-making: precise, dense, clean, full of detail.

### When to use

- Show a whole process end to end (the full value chain in one image).
- Show an org or industry as a town of departments.
- Show a user journey across a map (onboarding, checkout, day-in-the-life).
- Show a system and all its cooperating parts running at once.
- Especially: when the article is about *simultaneity* — "everything happening at once", "a busy morning", "launch day", "live system". A flowchart cannot show this; a panorama can.

### Composition rules

- Top-down or shallow high-angle. No strong perspective. Think "illustrated map", not "photograph".
- Divide the town into districts/streets that mirror the cooperation's structure (an input street, a processing square, an output quay).
- Every shop, vehicle, and square has an animal doing something — smoke from chimneys, flags on shops, parcels on carts.
- One focal cooperation zone (e.g. the central square where the main handoff happens); peripheral simultaneous work around it.
- Signposts at crossroads label the districts; shop signs label the roles.
- Warm palette, dense but legible. Generous but never empty — a panorama must feel populated.

### Signature tells

- a hot-air balloon or a church spire or a town-hall clock tower as the visual anchor
- a river or main street running through as the spine
- smoke from every chimney = the whole town is alive
- many small animals, each busy, none decorative

### Reject if

- it becomes a cluttered village with no focal zone
- it becomes a flat map with no life (no animals, no action)
- the cooperation isn't readable as a path through the town

## Cutaway Building / 剖面楼模式

A building cut open to show each floor and each window as a work station, with one animal on the job inside each. Interior detail is visible: pipes, shelves, ladders, gears, stockrooms. The front wall is removed (or partly removed) so the reader sees inside. **This is the signature Scarry-style page** and the most powerful mode for "what happens inside X".

### When to use

- Show a system as a tower of layers (UI → logic → data → storage).
- Show an org as an office building (each floor a department).
- Show an order/process flow as a multi-floor workshop (each floor a stage).
- Show "what's inside X" — a product, a stack, a pipeline, a body.
- Show hidden internal cooperation that a flat diagram hides.

### Composition rules

- The building stands in the center, front wall cut away. 3–6 floors visible.
- Each floor is one role / stage / layer. A hand-painted floor plaque labels it.
- One animal works at each floor, mid-task, with its tools visible.
- Vertical connections show the cooperation: a dumbwaiter, a ladder, a pulley, a chute, pipes — something physically carries work between floors.
- Optional: a cutaway street or foundation below showing what feeds the building; a rooftop showing the final output (a flag, a finished product, smoke).
- Keep the building readable: don't overcrowd a floor. If a floor has many sub-roles, show 1–2 and label the rest.

### Signature tells

- the cutaway wall edge (a zigzag or clean vertical cut)
- visible interior detail: pipes, gears, shelves, a ladder connecting floors
- one animal per floor window
- a hand-painted floor plaque (一楼/二楼/三楼 or 输入/处理/输出)
- a rooftop flag or smoke showing the building is "running"

### Reject if

- it becomes a stack of flat labeled boxes (that's an org chart, not a cutaway)
- the floors have no visible interior detail
- there is no physical connection between floors (no ladder, chute, pipe, dumbwaiter)
- the cutaway looks like a 3D render instead of a hand-painted illustration

## Two-Page Spread / 对照页模式

Left page and right page showing the same town drawn twice, with one thing different. The two pages share the same buildings, street layout, and cast, so the eye instantly locks onto the single delta. A shared axis (a river running across both pages, a road continuing from left page to right page) ties them.

### When to use

- Compare two ways of working (old vs new, manual vs automated, chaotic vs orderly).
- Show before vs after (a transformation across a whole town, not one object).
- Compare plan A vs plan B (two strategies, two architectures).
- Show a contrast that is about the *whole system*, not one part.

### Composition rules

- Both pages use the **same** town layout — same buildings, same streets, same district positions. Only the cooperation / state / density differs.
- A shared spine crosses both pages (a river, a main road, a railway) so the eye reads them as one continuous world.
- The difference must be **the cooperation itself**: one page has a bottleneck / chaos / waiting / a broken handoff; the other has smooth flow / parallelism / clear gates.
- Label each page with a banner (旧做法 / 新做法, 混乱 / 井然, 方案A / 方案B).
- Highlight the delta with a small callout flag or a circled zone on each page.

### Signature tells

- the gutter (center fold) between two mirror towns
- a shared river or road crossing the gutter
- identical buildings, different busy-ness
- a banner over each page

### Reject if

- the two pages show different towns (the comparison is lost)
- the difference is decorative (colors changed, but cooperation is the same)
- there is no shared axis tying the two pages

## Trade Close-up / 职业特写模式

One animal, one trade, in focus — its tools laid out around it, its action mid-motion, its output labeled. Like a single-page spread from a storybook devoted to one job. The background is a simplified workshop or street corner, not a full town.

### When to use

- Answer "what does a ___ actually do?" (what does a data engineer do, what does a PM do).
- Zoom in on one station of a larger town for depth.
- Show the tools / actions / outputs of a single role as a labeled kit.
- Introduce a role that will appear in a larger series later.

### Composition rules

- One lead animal, centered or slightly off-center, mid-action.
- Around it, a radial or grid layout of its tools and outputs, each labeled (a labeled kit).
- A simplified workshop / corner background, not a full town.
- Optional small inset showing where this role sits in the larger flow ("上游: ___, 下游: ___").
- The action is the focus: the animal is rolling dough / forging / inspecting / driving, not posing.

### Signature tells

- one big animal, many labeled tools around it
- a "kit" feeling — like a labeled diagram of a profession
- the action mid-motion (flour in the air, spark from the forge, parcel in mid-handoff)
- a banner with the trade name

### Reject if

- it becomes a generic mascot portrait (animal posing, no tools, no action)
- the tools are unlabeled
- there is no action (a static portrait is a sticker, not a close-up)
- it looks like a flat icon grid of tools instead of a hand-painted scene

## Choosing the Mode

Quick decision guide:

| The article is about... | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| a whole process / system / value chain / simultaneity | **Panorama** |
| "what's inside X" / a stack of layers / an org chart | **Cutaway** |
| two ways / before-after / plan A vs B | **Two-Page Spread** |
| "what does a ___ do" / one role in depth | **Close-up** |

When unsure, default to **Panorama** for breadth and **Cutaway** for depth — they are the two workhorses. Use **Two-Page Spread** and **Close-up** when the article clearly demands comparison or zoom.

A series may pair modes (see `series-and-chaining.md`): a Panorama opener establishes the world → a Cutaway drills into one building → a Close-up zooms one role → a Two-Page Spread resolves a comparison.
